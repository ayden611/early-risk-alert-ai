Pilot purpose

Evaluate whether authorized healthcare professionals can understand the review context and use it to support patient prioritization and workflow visibility. A pilot evaluates its agreed scope; it does not inherit a clinical-benefit or workload-reduction result from retrospective analysis.

Suggested measures below are planning options, not achieved results or new release requirements.

Evidence status

Canonical internal evidence accepted · Public numerical release on hold

The August canonical MIMIC-IV and eICU reverification records state ACCEPTED_CANONICAL_INTERNAL_EVIDENCE. Their public-release status remains HOLD. This page presents a qualitative summary and does not publish performance figures or grant numerical public-release clearance.

Public numerical evidence remains withheld pending the dated release-clearance decision required by the existing claim-content adjudication. Rebuilding this page does not itself change that status.

Suggested pilot measures

  • Alerts or reviews per patient-day within a clearly described observation period.
  • Visibility and reviewer understanding of priority tier, primary driver, trend direction, queue rank, and Timing context.
  • Completion and interpretation of workflow-state actions.
  • Reviewer feedback on how context supports their review.
  • Adherence to the decision-support boundary, including no autonomous escalation or treatment direction.

These measures need site-specific definitions before they can be interpreted. No numerical target or outcome is asserted here.

Existing pilot planning topics

The pilot planning discussion covers the participating site and clinical sponsor, scope and unit access, authorized users, IT/security contact, data-use pathway, and decision-support-only framing. This guide does not record a site’s approval or change an existing approval process.

Read the evidence in context

The Model Card describes intended use and the evidence limits. The Evidence Packet distinguishes internal acceptance from public release. Historical outcome-proxy, harmonized clinical-event, and subcohort findings are not interchangeable and do not set pilot success targets.
